    Scope and assumptions
    • Variables have a type and a distribution; labels such as treatment or outcome impose nothing. Any directed acyclic graph is allowed.
    • An arrow adds the parent, times its coefficient, to the child’s predictor on the scale shown in its equation; interactions add products of two parents.
    • Survival variables are latent event times. A cutoff or another variable as the censoring time adds observed-time and event columns; shared parents make censoring dependent.
    • Independent-observation designs generate independent rows. Structured designs retain subject and cluster variables across visits, with explicit shared effects, residual correlation and lagged relationships.
    • Power simulates whole studies from the graph and analyzes each with the method shown above. An effect to detect recalibrates one arrow for the run; the drawn graph is kept. Power is conditional on the generating distributions, effect sizes, dependence and observation process; Monte Carlo intervals describe simulation error only.
    • Models stay in this browser and can be exported as JSON; datasets download as CSV.
    Study or questionAvailable analysisLimits to check
    Independent continuous, binary or count outcomes, including heavy-tailed (Student t), proportion (Beta) and overdispersed (negative binomial) onesRegression, g-computation, AIPW and TMLE; quasi-likelihood with sandwich errors for Beta and negative binomial coefficientsTMLE requires binary treatment.
    Unmeasured confounders, error-prone measurements, hidden classesUnmeasured variables are simulated but left out of every analysisThe results show the bias they leave; no sensitivity-analysis estimator or instrumental-variable analysis.
    Relationships of unknown formSmooth random functions in the generator; TMLE or AIPW with a Super Learner (cross-validated ensemble) and cross-fittingFlexible estimation needs enough data for the shapes; wiggly functions in small studies leave bias. No Super Learner for the g-formula, survival or repeated-measures analyses.
    Effect modificationWald test of the compared variable’s interaction termsConditional regression test; plan for a larger sample than for a main effect.
    Complete repeated or clustered outcomesMMRM, LMM, robust trajectory means; baseline-treatment TMLETMLE targets a prespecified subject summary; cluster folds and inference preserve dependence.
    Missing visitsMMRM for monotone dropout; LMM for intermittent visitsModel and missing-at-random assumptions matter. TMLE with dropout is not implemented.
    Rare events, weak overlap, few clustersEditable stress-test scenarios and diagnosticsNo guarantee of calibrated inference. Compare failure rates, bias, null rejection and coverage.
    Single-event survival and censoringCox, Weibull, survival probability and RMST analysesNo survival TMLE, competing risks or recurrent-event estimator.
    Changing treatment, dynamic rules, crossover or adaptive trialsNo dedicated DAG power estimator yetRequire design-specific estimators and error-control rules; not covered by baseline-treatment TMLE.
